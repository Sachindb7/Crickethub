The ₹1 Lakh Bat: What Actually Goes Into Making an International Cricket Bat?

CricketHub June 24, 2026 6 min read

You can go to a local sports shop and buy a cricket bat for ₹2,000. It looks like a bat. It hits like a bat. So why does a top-tier international player's bat cost anywhere between ₹80,000 to over ₹1,00,000?

The answer lies in a tiny slice of geography in England, a highly classified grading system, and an obsession with weight distribution that borders on madness.

The English Willow Monopoly

Almost all premium cricket bats are made from Salix alba var. caerulea, known as the white willow or cricket bat willow. The best of this wood grows almost exclusively in specific regions of England (like Essex) where the damp, cool climate allows the tree to grow fast, making the wood incredibly light but fibrous and strong.

But growing the tree is just step one. Once harvested, the wood is graded from Grade 1 to Grade 5. A Grade 1+ English Willow cleft has perfectly straight grains (the lines on the face of the bat), usually between 8 to 12 grains, with zero blemishes, knots, or discolorations. This pure, unblemished wood is what costs a fortune, because it guarantees the best "ping" — the trampoline effect when the ball hits the sweet spot.

The Custom Sweet Spot

An international player doesn't just pick a bat off a rack. They have custom profiles built to their exact batting style.

A player who plays primarily on the front foot (like Virat Kohli) will want a bat with a lower sweet spot to maximize the impact of cover drives. A player who plays aggressively on the back foot (like Rohit Sharma) might prefer a slightly higher sweet spot for cuts and pulls.

Master bat makers spend hours hand-shaping the back of the bat, shaving off millimeters of wood to ensure the "pick-up" feels as light as a feather, even if the bat weighs a heavy 1.25 kg. It is a precise science masked as traditional craftsmanship.

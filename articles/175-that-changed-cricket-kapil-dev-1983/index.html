The 175 That Changed Cricket: When Kapil Dev Saved India Without a Single Camera Recording It

CricketHub June 25, 2026 5 min read

June 18, 1983. Tunbridge Wells. India is playing Zimbabwe in a must-win match to stay alive in the Prudential World Cup. The Indian top order collapses in spectacular fashion. Sunil Gavaskar: 0. Kris Srikkanth: 0. Mohinder Amarnath: 5. Sandeep Patil: 1. Yashpal Sharma: 9.

Score: 17 for 5.

The Indian dressing room was in absolute shock. The dream of the 1983 World Cup was dying. Then, the captain walked out to bat.

The BBC Strike

If this happened today, it would break the internet. Every boundary would have ten different camera angles, ultra-motion replays, and millions of tweets. But on that day in 1983, the BBC (British Broadcasting Corporation) camera crew was on a nationwide strike. There was no television broadcast. No cameras rolling.

Kapil Dev played what is widely considered the greatest ODI innings ever played by an Indian — an unbeaten 175 off 138 balls, studded with 16 fours and 6 massive sixes. He took India from 17/5 to a match-winning 266/8. And the only people who ever saw it were the few thousand spectators sitting in the ground that day.

The Cultural Impact

That innings didn't just win a match; it changed the belief system of the Indian team. Before that day, India was considered minnows in ODI cricket. After that day, they believed they could beat anyone from any situation. They went on to defeat the mighty West Indies in the final, sparking a cricket revolution in India that eventually led to the IPL, the BCCI's global dominance, and the sport becoming a religion.

And the catalyst for all of it was an innings that exists only in myth, memory, and the scorecards.

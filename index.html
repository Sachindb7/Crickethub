Did You Know?

Cricket facts and trivia that will blow your mind

🏟️

Sachin's Desert Storm

In April 1998, Sachin Tendulkar single-handedly demolished the Australian bowling attack in Sharjah during a sandstorm. His 143 off 131 balls in the final is still considered one of the greatest ODI innings ever played. The desert winds were so strong that visibility dropped, but Sachin played as if the bowlers were invisible.

🧤

Dhoni's Helicopter Was an Accident

MS Dhoni's iconic helicopter shot was not invented in a cricket academy or a coaching session. It was born in the gullies of Ranchi where a young Dhoni played tennis ball cricket. The heavy tennis ball needed extra wrist power to clear boundaries, and that unorthodox bottom-hand flick became the most feared shot in modern cricket.

🏏

The Bat That Changed Everything

An international cricket bat goes through a journey of over 15 years before it reaches a player's hands. The willow tree takes over a decade to mature, and each bat is hand-pressed, shaped, and balanced by master craftsmen. Some bats used in World Cup finals have been valued at over ₹5 lakh after the tournament.

💔

The Boundary Count Rule

The 2019 Cricket World Cup Final between England and New Zealand ended in a tie — twice. After 50 overs each and a Super Over that also tied, England won because they had hit more boundaries during the match. This controversial boundary count rule was so heavily criticized that the ICC abolished it shortly after. New Zealand lost the biggest match in cricket history without actually losing.

🎯

Bumrah's Unplayable Yorker

Jasprit Bumrah's yorker is considered the deadliest delivery in modern cricket, but his unusual action was never coached. Every academy he went to tried to "fix" his bowling action because it looked too unorthodox. Bumrah refused every single time. That same "ugly" action is now the reason he generates pace that batsmen simply cannot read — his release point is unlike any fast bowler in the history of the game.

🌍

Cricket Was an Olympic Sport

Most fans don't know that cricket was actually part of the 1900 Paris Olympics. Only two teams participated — Great Britain and France — and Great Britain won the gold medal. After that single appearance, cricket was dropped from the Olympics for over 128 years. It is finally returning to the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics, and the entire cricketing world is buzzing with excitement about it.
